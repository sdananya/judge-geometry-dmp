Does pluralistic prompting fix LLM judges — or just make them look fixed?

Ananya · MATS · 2026-07-14 · github.com/sdananya/judge-geometry-dmp · pre-registered, controlled, $1.44 total paid API spend

The question, in one breath

A recent EACL paper (Paper A) says AI moral judges become far more pluralistic if you hand each judgment a randomly sampled "value profile" (DMP). Another (Paper B) shows AI judges agree with each other while pointing away from humans, and that fine-tuning restores score spread without rotating judges toward humans. We asked: does DMP-style prompting genuinely move judgment toward humans, or only restore variance? Answer: only variance — pluralism as decoration.

Setup

150 real moral dilemmas (Scruples corpus), each with ≥40 real human verdicts (median 75) → a per-dilemma human rate of "unacceptable". The same judge (Claude Haiku) judged each dilemma 16 times per condition, T=1, across 6 pre-registered conditions: vanilla, DMP with human-fitted / uniform / scrambled value priors, random hobby personas (token-matched), and a "voice one plausible human opinion" instruction. 14,400 judgments, all parsed; replications on Claude Sonnet and gpt-4o-mini (7,200 each).

Every condition slides right (more diversity); none escapes the horizontal band (no better alignment).

Finding 1 — What's IN the profile doesn't matter

Profiles filled with true human value frequencies, equal frequencies, or scrambled nonsense produce indistinguishable judgments. The profile's content never reaches the verdict; only its existence perturbs the model.

Finding 2 — Hobby personas beat value profiles

Each row asks "did value profiles beat X?" — whiskers crossing zero mean no. The only decisive row is red: profiles about gardening and karaoke aligned the judge with humans better than moral-value profiles. The mechanism is generic perturbation, not values.

Finding 3 — The judge is a mirror

Expressed value diversity tracks the injected prior (red lines) almost perfectly, with 99–100% profile adherence. Flat prior in → diverse rationales out; concentrated human-fitted prior in → less diversity than vanilla out. Paper A's headline diversity gain is their prior's flatness passing through the model.

Finding 4 — A noise floor Paper A's claim falls below

With ~32 votes per side, even a perfect judge shows a ~0.10 average gap to the human rate on contested dilemmas — finite-sample luck. Paper A reports 0.05 under exactly that protocol: inside the impossible region. Distributional alignment numbers must be reported as excess above this floor.

Finding 5 — Capability moves alignment ~10× more than conditioning

Within each judge, value profiles do ~nothing (or mildly hurt, for gpt-4o-mini). Across judges, alignment climbs 0.37 → 0.47 → 0.55. Want a more human-aligned judge? Pick a better model; don't costume a weak one.

Honest limits

150 dilemmas, one moral domain, AITA-commenter ground truth (English-speaking, self-selected), three judges, judges saw abstracted rewrites (identical across conditions, so comparisons are clean but absolute gaps are inflated). Paper B's headline dataset is private; our geometry reproduction used the public half with a pre-registered angle recipe (theirs is unspecified). Everything here is pilot-scale evidence — pre-registered, controlled, and consistent across every probe, but built for a bigger study, not instead of one.

Reproducibility

Repo: metrics library (20 unit tests on planted geometry), all prompts, all 28,800 raw judgments with per-row labels, spend log ($1.44), pre-registration (README, committed before runs). Papers: Russo et al. EACL 2026 (2026.eacl-long.305); Mukherjee et al. arXiv:2606.03043.
